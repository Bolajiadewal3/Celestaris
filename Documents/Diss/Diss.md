Abstract
========

In modernity, sporting organisations – as businesses - rely on online
platforms to convey statistical information for consumers. The
learnability of such sites is essential to understanding and further
consumption. With a lack of published research on the topic, an
investigation into the impact of website design methodology has been
conducted: determining usability factors that affect learnability.

A mixed-methods approach of quantitative before-and-after questionnaires
and qualitative Likert and open-ended surveys was employed to gauge the
factors affecting website design satisfaction and quantifiable
learnability. Adopting an amalgam of pragmatist and interpretivist
views: the research is grounded in business theory – regarding consumer
decision making and brand.

The research finds that a hypothesis on the correlation between
usability heuristic inclusion and learnability is quantifiable – with
caveats pertaining to the accuracy of learned information. Also found
are the roles that visual complexity and readability play in subjective
opinion, alongside perceptions on the inclusion of key usability
features – such as closure and minimalism.

While the research confirms the hypothesis: the size of the test cohort
does not allow for definitive conclusions on the wider population
(normal distribution). In accordance, further research opportunities
arise to investigate the links between learnability and accuracy of
information as well as the employment of more websites to eliminate
prior brand exposure.

Practically, this research corroborates the application of usability
heuristics to decidedly increase the readability and learnability of
websites. Additionally, it provides evidence for the positive role of
gradual information exposition when designing information systems. This
investigation holds credence to both sporting businesses and user
interface enthusiasts. The implications on information system design are
discernible, with an avenue into the exploration of learnability and
website design within the business world that has been seldom
investigated in prior research.**\**

# Contextualisation

## Overview

What role does website design methodology play in the operation of the
National Football League? A business perspective must be employed to
contextualise this investigation:

The <u>National Football League</u> \[NFL\] operates as a business with
two major revenue streams: entertainment production and gambling (Chien,
2021; Eckstein, 2023; Quinn, 2012, p. 55; NFL Economics, 2022).

## Entertainment

The sale of entertainment within the NFL is multifaceted via (Chien,
2021; Eckstein, 2023; Quinn, 2012, p. 55; NFL Economics, 2022):

- <u>Name, Image and Likeness</u> \[NIL\] merchandising

- Brand sponsorship deals

<!-- -->

- Television broadcasting contracts

- Stadium ticketing

The NFL’s major revenue stream is their television media contracts, with
ticketing as the other large income source. Each team then individually
sells their media rights, increasing profit (Chien, 2021; Quinn, 2012,
p. 55).

The first two factors benefit from team and player reputation, persona
and outward-image, with popularity acting as a factor in revenue and
sales (Goldman, 2011). All the options, alongside gambling, benefit from
on-field player success: NIL and brand endorsements both correlate to
popularity that is generated from on-field performance and consistency,
while television and ticketing contracts and pricing, respectively, both
fluctuate based on team and thus player success during the season
(Quinn, 2012, pp. 66, 97). Moving forward, off-field reputation will be
mentioned but irrelevant to the contextualisation of appropriate website
design.

Gambling is different, with statistical data being utilised to place
bets and estimate victory chance (Eckstein, 2023; Quinn, 2012, p. 129).
While these are aimed at fans and outside marketing, the NFL also
internally utilises statistical data (Quinn, 2012, p. 137):

- To negotiate salaries in-season and restructure them in the offseason

- To objectively indicate player success and team contribution

With the average career spanning 4 years, it is vital for teams to
select the best available talent to maximise season success: by
employing statistics.

## Business models

Given this, a collation of two business models can further contextualise
superb website design’s necessity in communicating NFL statistical data.
Brand, simply, encapsulates the needs of this investigation and thus
several definitions must be defined according to K.L. Keller’s research
(Keller, 1993):

- Brand identities are terms, names and symbolic representations of
  goods and services

- A brand is an amalgam of the aforementioned identities

<!-- -->

- Recall is the retrieval of information from one’s memory

- Recognition is the ability to discern a stimuli after exposure

The first model – the brand equity model - is, critically, a methodology
for evaluating public memory’s perceived value of a brand (Huang, 2023).
Two proponent definitions can be discerned from literature, Keller’s and
Aaker’s, and an amalgam of the two shall be employed to explain the need
for above adequate website design. Aaker’s brand identities are (Aaker,
1991):

> *brand assets and liabilities linked to a brand, its name and symbol
> that add or subtract from the value provided*

In conjunction, Keller introduces brand awareness and association.
Awareness is the conjoining of recall and recognition to identify a
brand in one’s mind, while associations are powerful attributes that can
alter one’s opinion of a brand (Keller, 1993). The collation of these
definitions is a robust “model” - [**Figure 1**](#_Ref142040377) - that
showcases the need for brilliant outward presentation: furthering the
NFL’s branding. A more enticing website, viewed by all facets of the
league and its consumers, could lead to a positive shift in brand
perception. Every aspect of the NFL’s entertainment franchise serves to
increase positive brand equity: and website design, while subtle, holds
weight in swaying consumer opinion, especially in the current
information age (Huang, 2023).

## The National Football League

### Application

In applying the introduced models, the NFL’s emphasis on statistical
tracking, concerning fans, is to allow a greater level of immersion and
raise interest in team performance. This is to entice fans to stay
within the NFL infrastructure, rather than seeking alternatives during
their active evaluation phase such as (Elzinga et al., 2009):

- The XFL

- The <u>United States Football League</u> \[USFL\]

- The <u>European League of Football</u> \[ELF\]

More broadly, the NFL’s display of statistics, contribute to its brand
image, with a need to, through empirical data, uphold an appearance of
(Quinn, 2012, pp. 55-56):

- Parity and fairness

- Excitement and lack of mundanity

- Competitiveness

- Peak athleticism

These pillars form the public’s brand awareness of the NFL. The ability
to recall key statistics or rough approximations of such, that support
the above image, can thus be aided by brilliant website design. In
coherence, it is within the NFL’s best interests to ensure that
consumers are satisfied by searches into individual player and team
success.

# LITERATURE REVIEW

## Overview

A plethora of literature exists on <u>human-computer interaction</u>
\[HCI\] and the role that information systems hold within the modern
world (Gounder & Ali, 2022; Hamid et al., 2020; Hasan et al., 2013;
Macakoğlu et al., 2022; Miniukovich et al., 2018; Venkatesh & Agarwal,
2006). It has been suggested through a multitude of studies that HCI
increases productivity and corrects errors within interface design
(Gounder & Ali, 2022; Kumar & Mohite, 2016; Kumar & Mohite, 2017).

HCI literature increased in publication over the past decade, with a
discernible surge in articles in 2022, possibly influenced by the
digitisation of education and government sites post-COVID-19;
elucidating the need for effective information systems.

popular due to the hazy definition of usability and; HCI’s

## Usability

Usability holds a number of conflicting definitions, and was often-time
self-defined through reviewed literature - [***Table
1***](#_Ref142040602). If otherwise, two major definitions were utilised
between the remaining investigations: the <u>International Standards
Organisation’s</u> \[ISO\] of 1998 or Jakob Nielsen’s postulation (ISO,
1998; Nielsen, 1994b).

The ISO defined usability as *the effectiveness, efficiency, and
satisfaction with which specified users achieve specified goals in
particular environments*.

Nielsen defined it as the conjunction between *learnability, efficiency,
memorability, errors and satisfaction*.

Collating the two and adding a third allowed for the consolidation of a
concept of usability that could be deemed acceptable across current
literature. Hasan et al. relayed Brinck et al.’s definition of usability
as *the degree to which users can perform a set of required tasks*
(Hasan et al, 2013). Thus, a conflagration yielded usability as:

> *The degree of task completion, within specific environments, that
> users can ascertain – measured via effectiveness, efficiency and
> satisfaction – aided primarily via increases in learnability,
> efficiency, memorability and satisfaction alongside error reduction.*

This definition was supported by Baughan et al.’s definition of
usability, which emphasised aesthetics; describing usability as a
collection of *visually complex ideas* to enable task completion
(Baughan et al., 2020; Yakunin & Bodrunova, 2022; Miniukovich et al,
2018).

While encapsulating previous research, a number of studies posited
counter-intuitive results concerning the achievability of short-time
task completion when faced with objectively aesthetic designs. A number
of such studies hypothesised and proved, that an attempt to increase
satisfaction via better aesthetic principles led to a surprising
decrease in cognitive effectiveness during task completion (Goh et al.,
2013; Hamid et al, 2020; Thielsch, 2013).

Said research failed to consider measuring information recall and its
dependence on site aesthetics, yet the aforementioned results allowed
for a lack of surprise if subsequent results yielded a negative
correlation.

## Evaluation methods

The evaluation methods presented in previous research held no cohesion
across the discipline, yet differing subject matter held internal
consistencies - [***Table 1***](#_Ref142040602). The most popular method
for data collection was undoubtedly heuristic evaluation, with
Likert-scale questionnaires being employed to collect said data (Alamadi
& Drew, 2017; Aripiyanto et al., 2022; Azwar et al., 2021; Chandra et
al., 2022; Ekşioğlu et al., 2013; Firdaus et al., 2022; Givi et al.,
2022; Medina et al., 2019; Saeed, 2019; Saleh & Hailmi, 2022; Thielsch
et al., 2013; Vollenwyder et al., 2023; Wijaya et al., 2021; Xiong,
2021).

A proportion of research that employed such scales developed
questionnaires based on the usability heuristics first described by
Nielsen in his seminal work on usability and website design; thus: a
commonality in theoretical foundation was found (Azwar et al., 2021;
Ekşioğlu et al., 2013; Saeed, 2019). It must be noted that Krawiec and
Dudycz postulated that this theoretical hallmark is inadequate, two
decades later, in covering all aspects of usable design, hence a
combination of Nielsen’s, Schneiderman’s and Tognazzini’s were
amalgamated based on their presentation of more modern heuristics,
following their exhaustive review (Krawiec & Dudycz, 2020; Nielsen,
1994a; Nielsen, 1994b; Shneiderman et al., 2018; Tognazzini, 2014).

<span id="_Toc144969892" class="anchor"></span>: Research methods
present in the reviewed literature

The aforementioned Likert-scales usually followed two forms:
heuristic-based, often-time directly using Nielsen’s criteria or;
following the <u>System Usability Scale</u> \[SUS\] (Brooke, 1996;
Nielsen, 1994b; Sauro, 2011). While other scales were present in
literature, such as: WEBUSE; these were not widespread and could be
deemed niche and preference based (Agustina et al., 2022; Gerhana et
al., 2022). To increase the universality of this investigation, the
aforementioned heuristic and SUS systems were considered, as opposed to
others.

Over a sixth of the research papers utilised unique, *other* methods to
conduct analysis of system usability. Similarly, online tools were
employed for a portion of studies to collect quantitative metrics for
statistical analysis, seen in [***Figure 14***](#_Ref142040526), While
efficient and reliable, these methods opposed this study’s aesthetic
focus, with said papers opting to treat usability as mere metrics
tracked by web-browsers and programming-language validation tools.

## Usability within e-business

Usability within the concept of business models has undergone adequate
academic scrutiny (Goh et al., 2013; Hamid et al., 2020; Hasan et al.,
2013; Saeed, 2019; Shi & Yuan, 2019). Goh et al., conducted a usability
study on Malaysian users to evaluate an e-commerce site (Goh et al.,
2013). While the study was limited in its provision of e-business
theory, it elucidated the importance of page layout and structure in
allowing for effective task completion under observation (Goh et al.,
2013). A differing approach was adopted by Hamid et al., employing
Nielsen’s heuristics and the SUS to subjectively evaluate 20 Pakistani
e-commerce websites (Hamid et al., 2020). The only consistently met
heuristic was that of effective minimalism in design, with the others
proving inadequate (Hamid et al., 2020). While the subsequent low SUS
scores supplemented the perceived poor usability of the sites, no
specific improvements were suggested within the study – a gap that may
be answered in subsequent research (Hamid et al., 2020).

While such studies provided useful insights into usability within
commerce, Shi & Yuan’s statistical study provided an excellent,
evidenced categorisation of factors that affect user satisfaction:
relevant and applicable to the current context. The results revealed
that site content, personalisation and emotional control held the
heaviest weight on user satisfaction, trumping ease of use and publicity
(Shi & Yuan, 2019). Publicity is not an issue for the NFL’s marketing
behemoth, and thus allowing for personalised and dedicated website
content would, empirically, lead to better recruitment of NFL fans into
their business model (Elzinga et al., 2009; Shi & Yuan, 2019).

## Usability within the sporting industry

From the review of published, academic literature on usability: no
identifiable sources dealt with the specific scenarios of either –
usability within the sporting industry or; usability as measured through
information recollection of webpage content. While usability studies on
e-commerce and e-business touched upon the implications of inadequate
web content; no studies delved into sporting or fan entertainment (Goh
et al., 2013; Hamid et al., 2020; Hasan et al., 2013; Saeed, 2019; Shi &
Yuan, 2019).

This provided a unique research nadir to be explored. Solitary subject
areas have been explored before, with an online manga reading system;
web-mapping site and; architectural website, all having been evaluated
against aesthetic design principles within the past decade (Faliagka,
2015; Saleh & Hailmi, 2022; Wang, 2014).

Information recognition is similarly an area that has experienced low
research interest (Krawiec & Dudycz, 2020). The memorability of website
contents as a metric of effective design was explored briefly by Azwar
et al., within a larger study on how Human Centred Design affects
usability performance (Azwar et al., 2021). Their suggestions following
their report were to improve usability via addition of unique
information on the page – however adding more text opposes previous
research that suggests page complexity contributes directly to cognitive
deficiency (Azwar et al., 2021; Goh et al., 2013; Hamid et al, 2020;
Thielsch, 2013).**\**

# Methodology

## Overview

An approach to ascertaining the key factors in allowing for better
website design was formulated to investigate what factors held weight in
swaying popular opinion towards certain website designs, to be employed
in the presentation of statistical NFL data to fans and teams alike. The
investigation formulated two new website designs – testing their
usability on an unexposed population, alongside usability inspections of
two prominent existing websites: NFL.com and Statmuse.com, to elucidate
the application of theoretical concepts and; highlight the omission of
such concepts and their impact on site opinion.

The research methodology employed was multifaceted due to the
combination of academic fields being investigated. While the focus was
on the design of an information system, various sectors converged to
fulfil the ascertained goal, such as:

- Information Systems

- Human-Computer Interaction

- Philosophy

- Psychology

Each held purpose and explicit use in the development and deployment of
this investigation.

## Scope

The research philosophy adopted was one of the pragmatist ontological
approach, commonly abstracted as the mixed-methods approach (Saunders et
al., 2007). The research question could not be restricted into a
positivist or interpretivist viewpoint, as one can both experience a
website through the senses, and quantify the usefulness of a website
through information recall (Saunders et al., 2007). Thus, both
qualitative and quantitative modes of assessment were amalgamated to
craft a reproducible methodology that warranted response.

This investigation followed a top-down approach, based on the following
hypothesis, abstracted from the NFL:

> <span id="Hypothesis" class="anchor"></span>*Simple, efficient
> presentation of complex statistics via website design should
> equivocate to quantifiably easier information understanding, and a
> qualitatively identifiable increase in consumer confidence*

To break this down:

- *Simple and efficient presentation … via website design –* The design
  of a website could be simplified via the application of academically
  scrutinised psychology-based heuristics

- *Complex statistics –* Complicated statistical data that at times
  warranted definition

- *Equivocate –* An expected correlation between the simplicity of the
  presentation and the ability to recall its contents

- *Information understanding –* Understanding the complex terminology
  present on the site

- *Consumer confidence –* A stated, interest in potential repeated
  visitation of the site

The presentation of complex information via design was a direct result
of adherence to usability heuristics – laid out later – while
information understanding could be measured as learnability. Thus, said
hypothesis was sub-divided into smaller, achievable research goals -

## Usability Inspection

Before new websites were crafted, NFL.com and Statmuse.com both
underwent brief usability inspections concerning an individual player’s
statistics. This player acted as a control across all four websites
mentioned within this investigation – the two existing ones and the two
crafted for this report. The player chosen was Justin Jefferson: a wide
receiver for the Minnesota Vikings, as his status as the statistically
best player at his position over the past season, ensured that his data
was accurate and present on both websites (Miller, 2023; Next Gen Stats,
2022).

The usability of the two existing websites was scrutinised, at base
level via John Brooke’s <u>System Usability Scale</u> \[SUS\] and; more
specifically, according to Nielsen and Schneiderman’s usable design
components (Brooke, 1996; Sauro, 2011; Nielsen, 1994a; Nielsen, 1994b;
Shneiderman et al., 2018).

## Testing procedure

For testing: groups were utilised, with two being involved in a
comparative research investigation.

The third group solely viewed the two websites – in alternating order
per person – for a length of no more than two minutes each, and
concluded on their favourite design of the two via the deliberate
question: *which page did you prefer and why?* The short viewing time
stemmed from research conducted that highlighted the extreme speed with
which participants form judgements on website design, when denied
exploration:

- In a study, 20 seconds was the average time spent viewing an entire
  web page before forming a judgement (Dahal, 2011)

- In another, aesthetic judgements were established after a mere 17
  milliseconds spent on a webpage (Tuch et al., 2012)

The webpages were preloaded, to reduce the possibility of latency
playing a role in design judgement, as noticed by the second study (Tuch
et al., 2012).

It should be noted that the selection and division of the groups was
randomised from a list of volunteers: to reduce potential bias; but this
was not guaranteed, as age range and social position may have affected
the initial crafted volunteer list. Additionally, a lack of revealing of
the sports-based nature of the study was paramount, in preventing
volunteers from holding a clear bias i.e. potential fans of American
Football, as opposed to representing more accurately the general
population.

The remaining two groups engaged in a qualitative survey ranking (from 1
to 5) a selection of heuristics in terms of their integration level on
the page and; a quantitative test following site viewing to gauge the
change in knowledge on key NFL terminology, present on each site. Each
group was allocated a website to explore and given five minutes to
immerse themselves in said page.

The order of the test’s questions were random per person, while the
questions remained the same, to prevent the last question’s definition
loss due to time elapsed skewing results. The investigation was thus
cross-sectional in time, with no need to view changing data. All three
investigations were conducted within a week, to avoid the possibility of
exposure prior to website viewing.

## Statmuse.com

Despite differing design methodology, Statmuse.com encountered the same
drawbacks -***[Table 10](#_Ref144613764).*** Statmuse emphasised colour
and sleek design, opting to utilise a search function to allow for the
selection of personalised data queries. While this allowed a personal
experience, both websites lacked definitions of key abbreviations,
rejecting wider engagement. This policy was altered in this
investigation’s website designs.

## Comparison

Comparing the two sites qualitatively, Statmuse’s vibrance was more
visually enticing. In addition, the lighter hued colour scheme -
***[Figure 26](#_Ref144613843) & [Figure 27](#_Ref144613846)***; more
closely matched the chosen player’s team’s colours – encapsulating a
more professional, end-user driven design (*Color Designer*, no date;
*Color Picker*, 2022). Both Nielsen’s and Schneiderman’s criteria, were
achieved to a larger – albeit subtle – extent by Statmuse, with a
clearer display of current system status and perceived user autonomy.

Both sites were commendable in their use of psychological principles.
While mundane, both utilised continual flow of sight in their
top-to-bottom structure, while Statmuse added recognisable icons – such
as to the search bar and; provided auto-completion suggestions via
overlay – reducing the options for the user, potentially to their
benefit (Inci, 2020; Lupton, 2008; Sherwin, 2014).

With these sites as references for below adequate design, new designs
were formulated to assess the impact of specific design choices on
usability and user experience. To further this, both sites lacked a
number of Tognazzini’s principles, that have been evidenced to increase
positive user experience (Tognazzini, 2014).

# CONCLUSIONS

The results of the website analysis lead to discernible conclusions
between the Better and Mediocre site. A number of overarching hypotheses
were formulated and concluded before and after the procedure of
experimentation:

| Code | Hypotheses |
|----|----|
| 
``` math
H^{a}
``` | Adherence to usability heuristics correlates to subjective satisfaction |
| 
``` math
H^{b}
``` | Adherence to usability heuristics correlates to learnability |
| 
``` math
H^{c}
``` | Adherence to usability heuristics correlates to personal preference |
| 
``` math
H^{d}
``` | A quantifiable difference in learnability due to website design methodology exists |
| 
``` math
H^{e}
``` | Learnability of a website correlates heavily with gained appliable knowledge |

<span id="_Toc144716950" class="anchor"></span> Major research
hypotheses – identical to [Table 2](#_Ref144613421)

The statistical tests conducted on the Better and Mediocre sites
revealed that the correctness of information recall could not be
directly related to the information presented on either site. More
investigation could reveal a clearer relationship between information
gain and accuracy: websites with unambiguous definitions against ones
that demand semantic inference. In this vein, $`H^{e}`$ could not be
accepted within the current investigation, nor explicitly rejected.

In comparison, the information gain alone from both sites was clear and
evidenced. Upon visitation, both cohorts gained information on the NFL
brand, whether correct or not. This insight could be applied to the
NFL’s websites: paying closer attention to the information present on
their webpages, as they are evidently remembered by visitors. Following
this, a quantifiable difference in learnability did exist between
differing website design methodologies. $`H^{d}`$ was thus confirmed,
with the caveat that said information may not be 100% accurate.

In conjunction, a number of inferences were discerned from the Better
Site’s correlation plot of prior knowledge, scores and information gain:

- An increase in self-proclaimed knowledge level accommodated an
  increase in initial score, yet reduced the potential for information
  gain dramatically

- A higher initial score correlated to a similar reduction in
  information gain potential

- Both weakly influenced the final score positively, while an increase
  in information gain seemed to facilitate a reduction in final score

The final point was, most likely, a false correlation; increased
information gain coincided with a lower initial score and level of
knowledge; with these acting as the true catalyst for lesser final
scores.

The correlation plot introduced an obvious relationship between prior
knowledge and initial score, yet also revealed a phenomenon that already
held credence in psychology: self-perceived intelligence on a subject
lead to a decreased potential to learn new or correct information –
regardless of the accuracy of either banks of knowledge (Dunning &
Kruger, 1999).

Contrastingly, an increased correlation between initial and final scores
for the Mediocre Site implied that information gain held less
effectiveness on the scores themselves. This was corroborated by a
reduced negative impact of prior knowledge on the information gain,
implying that less information was gained overall in viewing the
mediocre site. Thus, adherence to usability heuristics could be deemed
to hold sway over learnability; with a difference due to design
methodology again being quantifiably presented.

Furthering the point on adherence to heuristics: the defining difference
between the two websites were the degree to which usability heuristics
were followed/achieved. Through inference, it was therefore apparent
that this difference in usability heuristic fulfilment contributed to a
change in learnability, subjective satisfaction and preference in
website. While the first two were verified by the usability analysis: as
differences in usability heuristics seemed to alter subjective ratings
of the page, coinciding with changes in learnability; the last factor
could not be quantifiably confirmed statistically – as hidden contextual
layers could have contributed to the subjective results of the
qualitative study.

Whilst not statistically confirming these hypotheses, said qualitative
research revealed a relationship between usability heuristics and
learnability. The readability of the website, as the most popular
deciding factor in website preference, was a direct consequence of the
adherence to the usability heuristics, elucidating the aforementioned
relationship.

Said usability heuristics seemed to, more clearly, influence overall
satisfaction when grouped into separate factors – such as a lack of
error diagnostics in both pages reducing perceived quality. Learnability
could have been influenced therefore by a difference in perceived
presence of action reversal, closure or shortcuts - with the mediocre
site foregoing the first two altogether. Shortcuts and their link with
information load has been documented and thus could certainly have
impacted both websites learnability, while a lack of action reversal
could have impacted it similarly, reducing the leniency to forget
brain-motor routines and instead store information relevant to the NFL
brand (Bidiu, 2018; Harley, 2019). In contrast, a lack of closure on the
page could have altered ones sense of correct answers: as buttons to
close sections could have better denoted quanta of information to be
remembered, and the differences between said information, rather than
forcing the user to remember all the page’s data (Miller, 1996; Moran,
2016).

In relation to the grand hypothesis – ***identical to page
[31](#Hypothesis)*** :

> *Simple, efficient presentation of complex statistics via website
> design should equivocate to quantifiably easier information
> understanding, and a qualitatively identifiable increase in consumer
> confidence*

The research presented an acceptance of said hypothesis, with the
disclaimer that easier information understanding does not explicitly
account for the accuracy of information encountered and learned: this,
in itself, requires further research.

# DISCUSSION

## PART I – FURTHER RESEARCH

Moving forward, the NFL, and sporting brands in general, should be
specific in their requirements for website design – as such sites
directly impact the knowledge of their consumers. These websites can
directly affect brand perception within the NFL, as information that is
learned may sway opinion positively or negatively.

Further research must be conducted to discern the relationships between
learnability and the accuracy of learned information, as this holds
importance within the sporting industry. In connection, the accuracy of
information before accessing websites should be assumed to be
non-perfect, as this study showcased a lack of perfection in perceived
knowledge from those that claimed to hold higher levels of prior
knowledge.

Thus, usability heuristics must be adhered to and maximised in such
adherence, to further the readability, visual appeal and professionalism
of sites and, by extension, learnability as well. This learnability
should – following more research – be geared towards ensuring the
absorption of accurate information, as opposed to any.

In solidarity, it was surmised that more research is needed in the
specific area of personal preference through visual cue. While studies
have showcased the impact of exposure time to websites as stimuli,
little has been conducted on the subjective ramifications of such
exposure on the preferences of the population. It could be revealed that
colour scheme holds more sway than readability when exposed to a larger
cohort, or that the colour scheme was in fact a sub-factor *within*
readability, as opposed to a factor alone. More research could elucidate
such relationships to a better degree.

## PART II – RESEARCH SHORTCOMINGS

The studies conducted encountered a number of statistical shortcomings
that could have impacted the accuracy of the conclusions, albeit not the
subsequent implications. A larger cohort could have better represented
the normal distribution of population – especially concerning age and
the effect that years of life may hold on perceived website readability
and learnability alike.

In conjunction, further research may require the splitting of cohorts by
their self-reported or tested degrees of accurate knowledge prior to
completion of activity, which could allow for better internal insight
into the factors that affect the differing groups.

Finally, in accordance with larger sample sizes, a selection of numerous
NFL brand associations – i.e. players – could be utilised in further
studies, to eliminate the potential for Justin Jefferson’s Name, Image
or Likeness producing bias within participants. An self-proclaimed
Minnesota Vikings \[Jefferson’s team\] fan would perform better in the
experiment with or without the aid of website design, due to their
higher assumed knowledge accuracy prior to testing.

## PART III – BUSINESS RAMIFICATIONS

In relating this investigation to a wider business setting, the
introduced models - [***Figure 3***](#_Ref142040444) - emphasised the
role that secondary brand associations can hold on influencing active
evaluation for consumers during their decision journey (Aaker, 1991;
Elzinga et al., 2009; Keller, 1993; Oliver, n.d.). It is therefore
evident that the results of this study provide clarity towards the ways
in which websites – as secondary brand associations that adhere to the
7C’s architecture, hold a larger impact on customer decision patterns
than often assumed – and that this impact reveals a requirement to
ensure above average learnability, and accuracy of learnable
information, on the websites of the National Football League, and wider
sporting statistic businesses.
